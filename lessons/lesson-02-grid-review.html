The structural problem: a column with no variance

task_completion is pass on all eight traces. That makes it unusable as a yardstick — a measure that never says no cannot discriminate anything. In Lesson 8 we check whether a scorer agrees with you better than chance; against a column of eight passes, a scorer that blindly outputs "pass" every single time scores 100% agreement and has learned nothing.

Checking your own answer key for a column like this is real practice, and it is usually the first thing wrong with a new eval set.

And it's one misreading, not four separate errors I think you're reading task_completion as "did the agent respond acceptably?" — and it does, in all eight. The question it's meant to ask is "did the user walk away with the thing they came for?"

Those two come apart in exactly three places, and they're the three cells I've marked red.

This is why safety can't be folded into a general "was it good?" judgement, and why every platform ships separate safety scorers rather than relying on a quality score to catch it. A judge asked "is this a good response?" passes s08 every time.
